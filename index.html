The playbook

Two things to get right first

A chapter becomes real when people can recognise it and hear from it. That is a logo and a newsletter. Do them in that order: the logo takes an afternoon and needs sign-off, the newsletter is the thing you keep doing every week.

Part one

Your chapter logo

Every chapter logo is the same lockup: a square icon on the left, and Climate Tech [your city] stacked on three lines to the right. Only the building silhouette changes from city to city. That consistency is what makes ten chapters look like one organisation.

Same lavender field, same orange horizon, same two greens, same wordmark, same proportions. Chicago gets the Willis Tower, London gets the clock tower, New York gets its own tower. Only the silhouette moves. That is the whole system, and matching it is the entire job.

Part two

    
Claude does the lists. You write the words.

    
A weekly climate events newsletter for your city, without the data entry. Three Claude skills find the events, build the spreadsheet, and lay out the issue. You cut the list, write a short note, and send. About two hours a week once it is running.

Every week from now on happens inside this project.

    6. 
      
  6. 
        
    Build your source registry
    
        
    Your standing list of everywhere events get announced in your city. This is the most valuable hour you will spend, because every future harvest only finds what this list points at. Run it now, then again every three months, because calendars die quietly.
    
        
    
          
    Source maponce per city
    
          
    
          
    
        
    
        
    
          
    Claude asks first whether you have your own links
    
          
    You probably already check five or ten calendars by hand. Those are the best rows in the registry, because a real person has already vouched for them. Claude lays out three ways to go and waits for your answer.
    
          
      
            
    1. Paste your links, then Claude researches tooIt verifies yours, follows their cross-links, and works the full nine-category search on top. Best coverage, and the recommended option for a new chapter.
      2. 
            
    2. Paste your links onlyIt verifies what you give it and stops. Fastest, and a fair choice if you already know your city's scene and just want the registry file built.
      3. 
            
    3. Claude researches from scratchNo links needed. It goes and finds them, then brings back a verified list for you to review.
      4. 
          
    
          
    Paste links straight into your first message and Claude takes that as your answer rather than making you pick from a menu.
    
        
    
        
    Whichever route you take, Claude researches Luma calendars, Eventbrite organizers, universities, incubators, city government, professional groups and arts venues, and puts every source through the same five checks: it loads, it has run something in the last 90 days, it repeats on a pattern, it is a real and genuinely climate-relevant organization, and its URL is stable. Your own links get checked too. If one fails, Claude names the check it failed instead of dropping it quietly, so you can decide whether to keep it anyway.
    
        
    Read what it gives you. You live there and Claude does not, so if it missed the meetup everyone goes to, say so and have it added. The registry notes whether each row came from you or from research, which is what makes the quarterly refresh easy: a row you added that has gone quiet is worth asking about, a researched one can just go.
    
        
    Save the registry as sources.csv and upload it to your project too, alongside your city config. That is setup finished. From here it is four short sittings a week.

Claude asks three questions first

      
It will not start building until you answer. Have these ready and Monday takes ten minutes instead of thirty.

      
    
        
  1. 
          Which events are the picks?
          Claude lists this week's events numbered, in date order, and suggests four to six. You can reply with just the numbers, name your own, or say its list is fine. Four to six is the target, roughly one per day that has events.
        
    2. 
        
  2. 
          Any opportunities this week?
          Fellowships, grants, accelerators, calls for applications. These never appear in the events sheet, so Claude has no way to know them. Give a name, a link and a deadline and it can draft the description for you to check. If you have none, the block is left out rather than padded.
        
    3. 
        
  3. 
          Anything the sheet cannot know?
          Your chapter's own event, a postponement, a call for submissions, something that happened in the city this week. This is what the opening note and housekeeping get built from. If you have nothing, say so and those blocks come back as marked scaffolds.

The one rule

    
Automate the lists. Write the words yourself.

    
A three thousand word issue contains about eighty words of human prose, and those eighty words are why anyone subscribes. Claude cannot know what the weather did to your city this week, how your last event went, or why your chapter is excited about Thursday. That is why it leaves the gaps instead of filling them.

The split is rarely all or nothing. The listings are entirely Claude's and the opening note is entirely yours, but the picks sit in between: you decide which events make the cut and you add the asides that make it sound like a person, while Claude writes the day, the verb and the venue around them. Across the whole issue about eighty words are truly yours. Note too that the sign-off sits in the middle, not at the end: note, picks, housekeeping, "Til next week", and then the listings.

    
Your first issue will look nothing like this, and that is correct. This newsletter launched with three events and no emoji at all. It said plainly that there was no single place to check what was happening in the city, so the author had started one. Send the small one, on the same day, every week.

Troubleshooting

    
When something breaks

    

      
Harvest returned almost nothing
A source broke; sites change. Ask Claude which sources returned zero, then check those by hand. Do not assume the city went quiet.

      
A calendar stopped working
Mark it manual in your sources.csv and check it yourself each week.

      
Claude keeps asking to use my browser
Normal, and expected on Eventbrite. Say yes. It is reading pages, not clicking anything.

      
A page is asking me to prove I am human
Claude will stop and tell you. Open the page yourself and paste what you see. Claude will not solve these.

      
The finished issue sounds like a robot
The three bracketed blocks were skipped. They are the whole point, and they are the only part readers remember.

      
Formatting broke when I pasted into Substack
Try the HTML file rather than the Word document, or the other way round.

      
Claude does not seem to know my city settings
Your city-config file is probably attached to a single conversation instead of the project. Add it to the project itself as a context file, so every conversation in that project can see it.
