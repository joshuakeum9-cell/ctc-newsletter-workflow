1 · Install the three skills

  1. In Claude, open Settings, then Capabilities, then Skills, and upload the three .skill files.
  2. Turn on code execution in the same panel. The skills need it to build your spreadsheet and document.

2 · Install the Claude Chrome extension

Some event sites hide their data from simple requests, so Claude needs to look at those pages in a real browser: yours. Search for "Claude for Chrome", install it, sign in with the same account.

3 · Fill in your city config

Open city-config-template.md and fill it in: chapter name, timezone, what counts as local, who signs the newsletter, your footer. Save it as city-config.md. Ten minutes. It gets uploaded to your Claude project in the next step.

4 · Create a Claude project

Make a new project (something like "Boston Newsletter") and upload your city-config.md to it. Everything weekly happens inside that project, so Claude always knows your city.

5 · Check that it worked

Before you leave this section, prove the install to yourself. In your Claude project, ask:

Which newsletter skills do you have available?

Claude should name all three: ctc-source-map, ctc-harvest, and ctc-assemble. If it does, you are installed, and Thursday will work.

If it does not: re-upload the three .skill files, confirm code execution is turned on in the same panel, then start a new conversation and ask again. Skills do not appear mid-conversation.

6 · Build your source registry

Your standing list of everywhere events come from. This is the most important hour you will spend: everything downstream depends on it. Aim for 20 to 30 verified sources, and redo this every three months, because calendars die quietly.

In your Claude project, run: 
Build the source map for [your city].

Read what Claude gives you. You live there and Claude does not; if it missed the obvious weekly meetup, say so and have it add them. Save the result as sources.csv and upload it to your project. Thursday Collect claude does this

Copy the prompt below and paste it into your Claude project. Claude sweeps every source on your list and hands you a spreadsheet. It collects everything with no filtering; that is on purpose, because collecting and judging are different jobs. If it asks to use your Chrome browser, say yes. It is reading pages, not clicking anything. About 15 minutes of your attention.

Harvest this week's events for [your city].

Save the spreadsheet Claude gives you. Friday's job is cutting it down.

Friday Cut you do this

Open yesterday's spreadsheet and delete rows. This is your job and nobody can do it for you: how many events end up in the newsletter is decided right here, and there is no target number. A good week is however many good events your city has. About 30 minutes.

What to cut

  • Anything outside your scope rule (it is written in your city config for exactly this moment)
  • Anything not really climate
  • Anything with a dead link or no date
  • If two rows are the same event from different calendars, keep the one with the better registration link and delete the other

What to flag

Mark three to six candidates for the picks. A convention that works: type PICK in a spare column for candidates, and just delete the rows you are cutting. What is left in the file is the issue; the PICK rows are what you will write about on Monday.

Monday Write claude assembles you write

One sitting, two halves, about 45 minutes. First, have Claude assemble the issue. Then write the three parts it hands back in [square brackets], because Claude cannot know them.

First: assemble

Copy the prompt, paste it into your Claude project, and attach your edited spreadsheet. Claude returns a Word document and an HTML page formatted for Substack. The listings, links, emoji, day headers and footer are finished.

Assemble the [your city] issue from this sheet.
The one rule

Automate the lists. Write the words yourself. A 3,000 word issue has about 80 words of human prose, and those 80 words are why anyone subscribes.

Then: write the words

Write these directly in the assembled document, replacing the [placeholders]. Do not let Claude write them; generated prose reads like generated prose, and readers can tell. Each scaffold below copies a starting structure so you never face a blank page.

Opening note

Two or three sentences about your city this week: the weather, something in the news, how your last event went. The archive's opening notes run short.

Hi all,

[Two or three sentences about your city this week. The weather, something in the news, how your last event went.]

Here are our picks for the week:

Picks

Bold day, verb, place or reason, then the linked title with its emoji. Join two events with "or", never "and". Real ones read like "make calls (and friends!)" and "an infrastructure tour on wheels". That is the difference between a newsletter and a list.

**[Day]**, [verb, place or reason]: [emoji] [linked event title]

**[Day]**, [verb, place or reason]: [emoji] [event] or [emoji] [event]

Opportunities

Fellowships, grants, deadlines. Keep a running list somewhere you will find it; most stay open for weeks, so carry them over issue to issue.

[Fellowship or grant]: applications close [date]. [link]

[Job or program worth a look]: [link]

Tuesday Send you do this

Check, paste, read, send. About 15 minutes.

First: the pre-send checks

Two minutes with the assembled issue open. Nothing here is automatic, and that is fine; you are the last gate.

  • Search the document for a square bracket. Nothing in brackets ships.
  • Scan the dates: nothing in the past, this week's events actually this week
  • Click two or three links at random; if one is dead, check its neighbours from the same source
  • Scan for the same event listed twice

Then: paste into Substack

  1. Open the HTML file (or Word document) Claude assembled.
  2. Make sure your three written blocks have replaced every [placeholder]. Nothing in square brackets ships.
  3. Select all, copy, paste into a new Substack post. Formatting and links come across.
  4. Read the whole issue once, top to bottom. You are checking voice and picks, not links.
  5. Send. Then log it below; thirty seconds, and future you will thank you.

Mark this issue sent

When something breaks

The 9pm questions.

Harvest returned almost nothing

A source broke; sites change. Ask Claude which sources returned zero, then check those by hand. Do not assume the city went quiet.

A calendar stopped working

Mark it manual in your source registry and check it yourself each week.

Claude keeps asking to use my browser

Normal. Eventbrite in particular blocks simple requests. Say yes; it is reading pages, not clicking anything.

A page is asking me to prove I am human

Claude will stop and tell you. Open the page yourself and paste what you see. Claude will not solve these.

The output sounds like a robot

You skipped the writing step. The bracketed blocks are the whole point.

Formatting broke when I pasted into Substack

Try the HTML file rather than the Word document, or the other way round.
